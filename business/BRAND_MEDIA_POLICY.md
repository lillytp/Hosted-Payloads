# Brand & Media Policy

Rules for social media, and for the operator's voice and image. The operator
has approved both as assets the business may use. This file says how — safely,
honestly, and without burning the operator's reputation.

## Social media

- **Approved as a channel.** Organic social (posting, replying, building a
  following) and paid social (see `BUDGET.md` advertising envelope) are both
  allowed.
- **Publishing is still gated.** Agents *draft* posts, scripts, and calendars;
  the operator (or a human they authorize) posts. Nothing goes live
  automatically.
- **No banned tactics — ever.** No engagement pods, bought followers/likes,
  fake accounts, mass-DM spam, ban-evasion, or posting against a platform's
  rules. Growth has to be real to compound.
- **One or two platforms to start.** Pick where the target customer actually
  is; don't spread thin.

## The operator's voice & image

The operator is willing to appear on camera / in audio if the orchestrator
judges it worthwhile. A personal face usually converts better than a faceless
brand — trust is the point. It also carries real cost (see risks below), so use
it deliberately, not by default.

**Real recordings (operator actually records):**
- Preferred form of personal-brand content. Agents may write scripts, shot
  lists, captions, and outlines; the operator records and reviews before it's
  published. Publishing stays gated.

**AI-synthesized likeness (voice clone, AI avatar, face/voice generation):**
- **Requires explicit per-use operator approval every time.** A blanket "yes to
  my image" does *not* authorize synthetic media of the operator. Each synthetic
  asset is its own approval.
- **Must be disclosed** as AI-generated wherever the platform or the audience
  would reasonably expect it, per platform rules and applicable law.
- **Never deceptive.** No synthetic clip of the operator saying things they
  didn't say, no fake endorsements, no putting the operator's likeness behind a
  claim that isn't true.

**Always:**
- The operator's likeness is only ever used for *this* business, never resold or
  repurposed elsewhere.
- Protect privacy: no home address, exact location, personal phone, family, or
  other PII in content.
- Any claim made in the operator's voice/image must be one the product can
  actually back up.

## Honest risks the operator has accepted (keep visible)

- **Permanence & reach.** Once published, voice/image content can be copied,
  re-uploaded, and scraped. It cannot be fully deleted later.
- **Deepfake exposure.** Public voice/face samples make impersonation easier.
- **Personal coupling.** Tying the brand to a real person raises reputational
  risk and makes the business harder to sell or hand off later.

If a piece of content meaningfully raises any of these, the orchestrator flags
it in the daily brief rather than treating "image is approved" as a blanket
green light.
